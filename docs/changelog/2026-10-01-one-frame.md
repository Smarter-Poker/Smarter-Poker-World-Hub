# One frame, not a frame broken across the middle

Dan, looking at the venue profile: "FRAMES SHOULD NOT BE THAT THICK, AND THEY
NEED TO BE ONE FRAME. NOT THIS BROKEN HALF AND HALF SHIT."

He was right, and it was not the artwork. The painted chassis is three
slices, a head, a repeating middle and a foot, and the head and foot both
declare their own full width. The middle did not. It was therefore the only
auto-width item in its container, so wherever that container resolved to
anything but stretch, the middle slice shrank to its content and slid off
the line its head and foot were holding.

Measured on the live site at 1440, before: the venue profile printed its
head and foot at x 220 width 1000 and its middle at x 244 width 976, so the
left rail stepped inward 24 pixels at the head seam and back out at the foot
seam. The discovery page was far worse, because its panels carry wider
content: a tool card printed 1012, 649, 1012 and a directory section head
1000, 627, 1000. Two rails where there should be one line, which is both the
break and most of the apparent thickness. Across the lobby, the discovery
page, a state directory, the more page and a venue profile, fifteen painted
frames were broken this way. All fifteen are now one frame.

The directory section heads had a second cause on top of that, and it was
mine. When those headings were put on the painted chassis last week, the
class carrying the old bare-header styling stayed on the panel root, so its
`display: grid` and two-column track landed on the chassis itself: the head,
the middle and the foot became grid items, the middle took the 1fr track and
the other two kept their own width. That layout now sits inside the panel
body, where the label, the heading and the hint actually live, and the CSS
hairline it also carried is gone, because the painted foot is what closes a
painted panel.

A slice of a frame is always the width of the frame.
