Operational Veracity: Identification of Permanently and Temporarily Closed Venues

A critical component of this audit is ensuring that users are not directed to "ghost rooms"—venues that remain in digital directories but have permanently ceased physical operations. The macroeconomic environment leading up to 2026 has been particularly harsh on specific geographic sectors, resulting in high-profile closures. Live poker operations possess inherently low profit margins per square foot compared to slot machines or proprietary table games. Consequently, as casino operators optimize their floors, poker rooms are frequently targeted for elimination.

The Las Vegas Market Contraction

Historically the global epicenter of live poker, the Las Vegas market has experienced a severe structural contraction. At the peak of the poker boom in the late 2000s, the city boasted approximately 60 active rooms. As of March 2026, that number has dropped precipitously to just 17. Casino operators have increasingly realized that the square footage required for a poker room yields a significantly lower return on investment compared to modern stadium gaming configurations or high-density electronic table game (ETG) layouts.   

The following Las Vegas venues, which may still be listed in broader historical databases, must be explicitly flagged as Permanently Closed:

Resorts World Las Vegas: Opened with immense fanfare in June 2021, the room was the first new Las Vegas casino to open with a poker room since 2009. However, the venue is slated for permanent closure by the end of March 2026. The space, located away from the main casino floor, failed to capture the necessary foot traffic and will be repurposed by management.   

Planet Hollywood: Despite three distinct attempts to relocate and revive the room—including a placement where stadium gaming sits today, another in the current ETG pit, and a final mezzanine level placement prior to the 2025 World Series of Poker—the room was permanently shuttered in January 2026. The space currently remains vacant.   

Poker Palace: A staple for low-stakes grinders, this venue closed on September 30, 2025. Following a property sale and extensive remodel, it is expected to reopen in 2026 under the new branding "Club Fortune North." However, live poker operations have been completely excised from the new business model.   

Sahara: The seven-table room suffered from devastating timing, opening in February 2020 just weeks before global pandemic shutdowns. Unable to recover momentum upon reopening, it permanently closed on November 16, 2024. The floor space has been entirely replaced by slot machines.   

Flamingo: The 11-table room was initially framed as undergoing a "temporary closure" in September 2021 to alleviate corporate staffing shortages during the delayed WSOP. However, Caesars Entertainment subsequently made the closure permanent in November of that year.

Distinctive Operational Models: Cash-Only and Tournament-Only Venues

While the vast majority of the 428 listed venues operate a hybrid model—utilizing tournaments as a loss-leader or marketing mechanism to drive foot traffic into highly profitable cash games—a distinct subset of venues explicitly rejects this approach. Platform databases must accurately tag these venues to prevent severe user friction, as a cash game grinder traveling to a tournament-only venue (or vice versa) results in a highly negative user experience.

Verified "Cash-Game-Only" Venues

The operational rationale for a cash-game-only model is mathematically straightforward: tournaments require immense logistical overhead (dedicated dealers who do not collect standard cash tips, clock management, floor staff for table balancing) and yield a fixed, single-time rake via the administrative fee. Conversely, cash games generate continuous, compounding rake every hand without the need to ever eliminate players from the building.

Black Eagle Country Club Poker Zone (Black Eagle, MT): This venue can be verified beyond a shadow of a doubt as a cash-game-only operation based on current operational data. The room explicitly states: "We play $2-$10 spread limit with No Limit overs Texas Hold'em. The game starts @ 7pm every day". There are no scheduled tournaments listed across any aggregator for this facility. Montana's unique tavern gaming laws heavily incentivize small, single-table cash operations over logistically complex tournament structures, making this model standard for the state.   

Hialeah Park Casino & Magic City Casino (Miami, FL): While these are massive facilities compared to Montana taverns, specific data tracking their weekend afternoon activities shows zero tournament table allocations. Instead, 100% of their operational bandwidth is dedicated to cash games. Hialeah Park focuses on $1/$2 NLH, $2/$5 NLH, and PLO, while Magic City focuses on $1/$2 NLH, $2/$5 NLH, and unique 2-4 Mixed/Omaha games. While they may occasionally run special events, their daily operational posture is overwhelmingly, if not exclusively, cash-centric to maximize the rake extraction from the Miami market.   

Verified "Tournament-Only" Venues

The inverse model—venues that refuse to spread cash games entirely—is exceedingly rare in the commercial casino sector but exists in specific regulatory or private club environments where state laws forbid the collection of a raked pot.

Pop's Poker (Richmond, VA): This venue is explicitly flagged by player verification as a tournament-exclusive operation. Player reports categorically state: "no cash games and only tournaments". The strategic rationale here often involves avoiding the complex accounting, security, and banking regulations necessary to manage a live cash cage, instead relying on the fixed-fee, closed-loop economy of tournament buy-ins to comply with local Virginia statutes.   

Strategic Implications and Platform Remediation Directives

The exhaustive analysis of the 428 active venues reveals a North American poker ecosystem characterized by extreme regional variance, divergent operational strategies, and rapid physical shifting. To ensure the platform remains the definitive source of truth for the industry, the following remediation directives must be implemented:

Algorithmic De-Duplication is Imperative: The platform must immediately implement a parent-child data structure for its venue logic. Venues like "Aria" and "Aria Casino" cannot exist as separate entities. By linking the specific poker amenity (e.g., "Bellagio Poker Room") as a child entity to the parent resort ("Bellagio"), the platform will consolidate user reviews, eliminate scheduling confusion, and drastically improve SEO routing.   

API Integration for Real-Time Status Verification: The manual flagging of closed rooms (such as the 17 rooms permanently shuttered in Las Vegas or the temporarily devastated WinStar facility) is unsustainable for a modern platform. The platform must actively sync with management software endpoints (e.g., TableCaptain or Bravo Poker Live). If an API fails to return live waitlist or dealer data for 30 consecutive days, the venue should be automatically shifted to an "Unverified/Pending Closure" status, triggering a manual review.   

Regulatory Nuance Tagging: Venues operating in unique legal jurisdictions must be tagged accordingly. Venues in Washington State must be explicitly tagged with "Spread Limit Regulations Apply" to manage the expectations of out-of-state players expecting true No-Limit games. Similarly, Michigan venues operating under the charity model (like Krazy Kopz) must be tagged with "Charity Room - Variable Hours," as their operational days are strictly dictated by rotating non-profit licenses.   

Granular Filtering Capabilities: The identification of pure cash-game venues (Black Eagle) and pure tournament venues (Pop's Poker) necessitates the implementation of hard UI filters within the platform's search architecture. Users must be able to instantly exclude venues that do not cater to their specific playing preferences.   

The commercial poker market is no longer a monolith dictated entirely by Las Vegas. It is a highly fragmented, regionalized network of mega-rooms offset by contracting legacy markets and specialized tribal/charity operations. Database integrity requires constant, forensic auditing to ensure platform users are routed to verified, active, and operationally appropriate venues.
